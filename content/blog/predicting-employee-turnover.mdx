## The real cost of turnover

Losing and replacing a mid-level manager costs on average 50–200% of their annual salary. That includes recruiting, onboarding, and lost productivity. Most organisations never calculate this precisely – which is exactly why they don't take it seriously.

<Callout>
The exit interview comes too late. By then the decision is made. The personality profile shows what the exit interview no longer can.
</Callout>

Some turnover cannot be prevented: life situations change, better offers arrive, careers take turns. But research suggests 60–80% of exits had a preamble – signs that were visible in hindsight, which nobody was watching.

## The three most common exit profiles

**1. The person–role mismatch**

The person is competent, but the role keeps demanding things that run against their natural way of working. Someone with a high <DimBadge code="E" label="Emotional stability" /> requirement mismatch in an intensely client-facing role won't perform well – and will eventually burn out.

This is not a question of ability. It is a question of fit.

**2. The person–manager mismatch**

The manager's and the report's personality profiles cause lasting friction. Between a high <DimBadge code="C" label="Conscientiousness" /> manager and a high <DimBadge code="O" label="Openness" />, low-C report, the source of conflict is structural: one values precision and process, the other flexibility and experimentation.

Neither is wrong. But if this dynamic isn't recognised and managed consciously, the tension will look like a personal conflict.

**3. The values mismatch**

The least visible but most damaging type. When a high <DimBadge code="H" label="Honesty-Humility" /> person works in a low-H culture – where politicking, information hoarding, and self-interest are the norm – a lasting values conflict develops. They won't leave immediately. But they disengage, withdraw, and eventually go.

## What can you do before hiring?

A personality profile is not a screening tool – and must not be used as one. But as a structured aid, it helps define what natural tendencies a role demands and where the fit challenges will be.

If you know at hiring time that a candidate scores a certain way on <DimBadge code="E" label="Emotional stability" /> and the role carries sustained high pressure, that knowledge shouldn't be used against the hire. It should be used to manage the load consciously during onboarding and day-to-day work.

## What can you do in an existing team?

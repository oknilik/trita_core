## Why talking early pays off

Losing a good person is one of the most expensive events in a smaller organisation's life – and a large share of leavers later say it was not inevitable. A departure rarely has a single cause: role, manager relationship, growth prospects, and the job market shape it together. (It is a myth that people “always leave their manager” – the research shows a more complex picture.)

<Callout>
The personality profile here is not a fortune teller but an agenda: it shows what is worth talking about before the decision takes shape in someone's head.
</Callout>

## What does a departure cost you?

The often-quoted 50–200% band is wide because the real cost differs by organisation. Three questions give you your own number: how many months does the seat stay empty? How much leadership time do selection and onboarding consume? When does the new hire reach the expected level of independence? Once you have run this calculation, retention conversations suddenly look like a very cheap investment.

## Three fit questions

**1. Do the role's demands and the person's preferences fit?**

The profile helps put into words the environment where someone works most naturally: many parallel conversations or long stretches of focus, tight process or broad autonomy. If the role persistently demands something different, that alone is not a problem – but say it out loud, and add a conscious counterweight. For example: protected focus time for the colleague worn down by eight calls a day; clearer structure for the one drained by ambiguity.

**2. Are the working rules between you and the team member clear?**

Most friction is not a personality flaw but an unspoken expectation. The profile shows where you may diverge – and three questions settle it: How detailed a plan do we need? When is it okay to deviate from the process? How do we flag risk to each other?

**3. Are personal and organisational values aligned?**

Values show up in everyday decisions: what we reward, what we let slide, how we treat each other under pressure. The profile gives this conversation a language – the answer comes from the organisation's actual norms, not from a score.

## What to watch in an existing team

A lasting change in initiative, participation, or feedback is always a good reason for a supportive conversation. No diagnosis needed: overload, a personal situation, or a workflow problem could equally sit behind it – the conversation is worth it in all three cases. Timing is the point: ask while it is still a conversation, not a resignation announcement.

## Would you use it for hiring?

<KeyInsight>
Do not use trita's development-focused profile to screen out candidates or as a standalone suitability decision. Selection use requires job-specific validation, fairness review, and multiple sources of evidence.
</KeyInsight>

Derive the role's requirements from structured job analysis. In selection, the structured interview, work samples, and experience play the lead – personality data can at most be a supplementary source, and only with an instrument validated for that purpose.

## Sources and limitations

Associations between personality traits and turnover are moderate: they show group-level tendencies and do not predict an individual resignation. That a leaver retrospectively sees their departure as preventable is not the same as prospective predictive accuracy. The 50–200% cost band is not a universal average – your own cost model is worth more. A gap between self-image and colleague feedback is a difference in perspective, not change over time; change can only be claimed from comparable, repeated measurement.

- [Zimmerman: meta-analysis of personality and turnover](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1744-6570.2008.00115.x)
- [Hom et al.: one hundred years of employee turnover research](https://doi.org/10.1037/apl0000103)
- [Kristof-Brown et al.: meta-analysis of person–environment fit](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1744-6570.2005.00672.x)
- [Gallup: preventable departures and replacement costs](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/646538/employee-turnover-preventable-often-ignored.aspx)
- [SIOP: principles for the validation and use of selection procedures](https://doi.org/10.1017/iop.2018.195)

*These studies describe average, group-level associations. A personality profile is not a diagnosis, does not prove an intention to resign, and does not replace the conversation with the team member.*

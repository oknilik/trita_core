## A profile does not determine team dynamics

A team's functioning cannot be explained by adding up individual personality scores. The task, leadership, role clarity, team history, and shared working agreements all matter.

<Callout>
A team heatmap is not a diagnosis. It is a map of where to ask for concrete experience and evidence.
</Callout>

Research suggests that both mean team traits and differences between members can be associated with performance. These associations are generally modest and context-dependent. There is no universal optimum for team size or personality composition.

## What does the heatmap show?

trita's heatmap displays team members in rows and dimensions in columns. Alongside individual results, it makes the distribution visible: where members are similar and where differences are larger.

**Low spread on a dimension.** Similar work preferences may be present. This can simplify coordination, but in some tasks it may narrow the range of perspectives.

**High spread on a dimension.** Different work styles may be present. This can add alternative approaches, but it becomes useful only when the team agrees how to make decisions, share information, and coordinate deadlines.

The distribution alone cannot tell us whether similarity creates a blind spot or difference creates conflict. Those hypotheses need to be checked against observable behaviour.

## Five possible patterns to discuss

**1. Higher mean Conscientiousness.** The team may favour planning and consistent execution. Ask whether this supports the current task and whether rapid replanning still has room.

**2. Higher mean Agreeableness.** Cooperation may be easier, but it remains important to check whether dissenting professional views are heard. A higher score does not prove harmony, and a lower one does not imply conflict.

**3. Higher mean Extraversion.** Verbal participation may be more active. Written idea generation and preparation time can help ensure that slower-to-speak perspectives are also included.

**4. Large differences in Honesty–Humility.** Members may hold different expectations about fairness, status, or self-interest. Scores do not establish politicking or unethical conduct; discussion should focus on concrete situations and shared norms.

**5. Diversity across several dimensions.** Diversity is not an automatic advantage. It may help when the task benefits from different perspectives and the team can integrate them.

## What should you look at first?

<KeyInsight>
Do not ask “who might be the problem?”. Ask “where might our ways of working differ, and what agreement would help us handle that?”.
</KeyInsight>

Review the mean, the spread, and more extreme scores separately. A “top 10%” label is meaningful only when a score is a percentile based on a documented norm sample. A 0–100 display is not automatically a percentile, and a small score difference may sit within measurement error.

In standard HEXACO, <DimBadge code="E" label="Emotionality" /> is not simply reversed neuroticism. Every heatmap label and direction must be tied to the technical definition of the scale used.

## A development conversation, not a label

The heatmap is useful when the team checks hypotheses against its own examples. A facilitated workshop can help create working agreements and complement individual development. It does not automatically replace coaching, performance evidence, or leadership observation.

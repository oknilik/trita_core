How a team works cannot be explained by adding up individual personality scores. The task matters, so do leadership, role clarity, the team's history, and the way shared working rules were formed. The aggregate profile is good for showing where it is worth asking about concrete experience.

## What do the team and the leader see?

After completing the questionnaire, each participant sees only their own personality profile. The report prepared for the leader and the team, approved by a consultant, shows an aggregate team picture: the team average per dimension, the bands of internal diversity, and a shared interpretation of both. The report contains no rows by name, and it does not show any other team member's individual score.

<Callout>
The aggregate team profile does not reveal where anyone stands. It helps the team talk about shared working situations instead of guessing about people.
</Callout>

Each team member's own view is more personal than that. They can compare their own values with the team's aggregate picture. That comparison concerns only them, and it gives no access to colleagues' profiles.

## Dynamics do not follow from the profile

The 2006 meta-analysis by Peeters and colleagues examined the relationship between team members' personality characteristics and team performance. For a team's average Agreeableness the figure was ρ = 0.24, and for average Conscientiousness ρ = 0.20. Both are weak associations. In plain terms, comparing many teams reveals a slight tendency. The performance of a single team cannot be predicted from it.

Bell's 2007 meta-analysis found associations of similar magnitude and emphasised the role of context. The 2024 pre-registered analysis by Van Dijk and colleagues examined the effects of personality diversity. The overall picture is that there is no optimal composition valid for every team. The right pattern depends on the task and the environment.

In practice this means the aggregate picture is best read as a collection of hypotheses. Every proposition has to be checked against the team's own examples. That step cannot be skipped.

## What do the team average and internal diversity mean?

Each row tells you two things: where the dot sits (the team average) and how wide the band is (the internal diversity). In the example above, the band on Agreeableness is narrow and the one on Conscientiousness is wide – the same team works similarly in one area and in several ways in the other.


**The team average** is one starting point of the shared profile. A higher average <DimBadge code="C" label="Conscientiousness" /> may suggest that planning and consistent execution matter in how the team works. It does not follow that everyone works the same way. The report rather raises the question of whether enough room remains for replanning when the situation changes.

Alongside a higher average <DimBadge code="A" label="Agreeableness" />, it is worth examining whether uncomfortable professional disagreements also get space in the smooth cooperation. And a higher <DimBadge code="X" label="Extraversion" /> average does not tell you who speaks a lot or a little. It directs attention to whether thoughts formed in writing get room alongside quick spoken reactions.

**The band of internal diversity** indicates how similar or different the response patterns behind the aggregate picture are. A wider band may mean several ways of working are present. The report deliberately does not show who sits where within the band. So the shared question is not „who is the outlier”, but in which situations this diversity may become important.

This matters especially for sensitive dimensions such as <DimBadge code="H" label="Honesty-Humility" />. The consultant's interpretation can start a conversation about fairness and about less visible work. It is not suitable for inferring anyone's intentions or integrity.

<KeyInsight>
The aggregate report does not show what anyone is like. It gives a shared vocabulary so the team can examine how it works in concrete situations.
</KeyInsight>

## What should you look at first?

Do not ask who might cause a problem. Ask in which situations your ways of working might diverge, and what rule you will handle that with. This framing decides what the report will be good for.

Look at the average and the width of the diversity band separately. You can only speak of a top ten percent if the score is a percentile relative to a documented norm sample. The 0-100 display is not in itself a percentile. A smaller point difference can fall within measurement error.

Pay particular attention to the <DimBadge code="E" label="Emotionality" /> dimension. It is not simply the inverse of neuroticism, and the emphases fall elsewhere. Tie every label and direction to the technical description of the scale in use.

## An example of the first thirty minutes

Suppose the report shows wide diversity on the Extraversion dimension. The tempting reading is that the quieter colleagues need to be drawn out. That is already a solution, when the problem has not even been established.

The usable first step is a question. „In the last five meetings, who gave their substantive view, and who did not?” The team can answer that with concrete examples. The answers reveal whether the unevenness of participation bothers them at all.

If it does, the experiment can follow. Written input before a decision, preparation time, or a round of questions at the end of a meeting. Look at whether it worked a few weeks later. The report provided the frame for that opening question.

## A development conversation, not labelling

The aggregate picture is useful when the team checks the proposed hypotheses against its own examples. A facilitated workshop can help form shared working rules, and it can complement individual development. It does not replace consulting conversations, performance data, or leadership observation.

You can read a practical example in our [anonymised team story](/blog/one-team-one-heatmap). It follows how a wide Extraversion band turned into written decision preparation.

## Limitations and sources

The research cited describes average, group-level associations. It is not suitable for predicting the behaviour of a particular team member or a team's future performance. The effect sizes are moderate and depend heavily on the task and the organisational context. The team average and the diversity band also carry measurement uncertainty.

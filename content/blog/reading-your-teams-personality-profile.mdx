## What does the heatmap show?

On trita's heatmap, rows are team members and columns are the six dimensions. At a glance you see two things: which way the team as a whole leans, and where individual differences are large. Research links both to team performance – but the real value is that it shows you what is worth talking about.

<Callout>
What the heatmap does best is show you where to ask about how your team works. The answers come not from the colours, but from the conversation they start.
</Callout>

**Where the colours are similar,** the team agrees quickly – this speeds up everyday work but can narrow the range of viewpoints. **Where the spread is wide,** several working styles live side by side – a resource, once you have clarified how you make decisions and align on deadlines.

## Five common patterns – and what to do with them

**1. High average Conscientiousness.** The team likes to plan and execute consistently. A strong foundation – the leadership question is whether there is room for fast re-planning when the situation changes.

**2. High average Agreeableness.** Smooth collaboration, little friction. Ask yourself: do the uncomfortable professional disagreements get voiced too? If harmony suppresses debate, a rotating “designated critic” role per decision can help.

**3. High average Extraversion.** Lively meetings, quick verbal reactions. Complement them with written idea collection and preparation time – so the more deliberate colleagues' views arrive before you decide.

**4. Wide spread on Honesty–Humility.** Different expectations about fairness and self-interest may coexist in the team. This is where explicit rules work best: how do we share the visible and the invisible work, and what counts as fair here?

**5. A team varied across several dimensions.** Many perspectives are present. This becomes an advantage when the task calls for different approaches and you have agreed how debates get closed.

## How to read it: average, spread, edges

Three glances, three questions:

- **Average** – which way does the team lean overall? This is the team's “default setting”: planning or improvising, aligning or working independently.
- **Spread** – how large are the differences on a dimension? Low spread promises quick agreement; high spread promises more perspectives – each has its cost and its benefit.
- **Edges** – who stands far from the team's core? They are not “the difficult one”: often they see exactly what the others miss.

For example, if five scores in a six-person team sit above 60 on Conscientiousness and one sits well below, that one colleague most likely experiences the process discipline as tightest. The first question is not “why don't they adapt” – it is “what do they see that we don't”.

<KeyInsight>
Don't ask “who might cause a problem?”. Ask “in which situations might our ways of working diverge, and what rule do we handle that with?”.
</KeyInsight>

## From heatmap to working agreements

The heatmap becomes useful the moment it turns into a conversation. A flow that works: the team picks one or two patterns, checks them against its own examples, then agrees on one or two working rules to try. For instance: “everyone gives written input before a decision”, or “we commit to the deadline together, everyone schedules their own milestones”. A month later, you review: did it work?

In a facilitated workshop this process gets a guided, safe frame – it complements individual development, coaching, and leadership observation rather than replacing them.

## Sources and limitations

Alongside the profile, a team's functioning is shaped by the task, leadership, role clarity, and the team's history – the heatmap offers hypotheses, not a diagnosis. The associations found in research are moderate and context-dependent; there is no universally ideal composition. A small score difference can sit within measurement uncertainty, and a 0–100 display is not by itself a percentile. The precise meaning of each dimension label is fixed by the scale's documentation – Emotionality, for example, is not simply reversed neuroticism.

- [Peeters et al.: meta-analysis of personality and team performance](https://doi.org/10.1002/per.588)
- [Bell: meta-analysis of deep-level team composition and performance](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17484544/)
- [Van Dijk et al.: registered meta-analysis on personality diversity](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10869-024-09977-0)
- [Official HEXACO scale descriptions](https://hexaco.org/scaledescriptions)

*These studies describe average, group-level associations. They do not allow confident prediction of a specific team member's behaviour or a team's future performance.*

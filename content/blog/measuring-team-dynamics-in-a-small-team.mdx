In a small team, dynamics are not statistics. With four people every relationship is unique, and one missing response removes a quarter of the picture. That is why measurement in a small team is built differently from a fifty-person organisation. This article describes what trita measures in a team of four to eight, where the thresholds are, and what not to read into the picture.

## Why is a small team different?

Size is a dynamic factor in itself. Wheelan studied 329 work groups in 2009 and found that groups of three to eight were at a more developed stage of functioning and proved more productive than groups of nine or more. Mueller's 2012 study showed the other side. In larger teams individual performance drops, and laziness does not explain it. Relationships get lost, because nobody can maintain a working relationship with everyone.

A small team has no such loss. In return, every relationship carries a lot of weight. If trust does not work between two people in a five-person team, that affects a tenth of all relationships. For measurement this is an advantage and a risk at the same time. An advantage, because the picture is sharper. A risk, because an individual answer is easier to trace back.

## The three thresholds that decide everything

trita's team picture can be read from three completions. From fewer profiles we do not compute a team pattern. The same threshold of three applies to every anonymous measure: the trust network, psychological safety and peer role feedback. The peer picture of a person appears only once at least three people have completed it about them.

<Callout>
The threshold of three is not a statistical nicety. It is a data-protection contract with the team: from two answers either party could guess what the other said. From three, that no longer works.
</Callout>

In a small team this threshold often means the whole team. In a four-person team the trust network appears for a member only if the other three have all completed it. That is why participation is the most important question at introduction. Our [article on introducing an assessment](/blog/introducing-psychometric-assessment) covers it.

## What does the trust network measure?

The trust round asks five questions about each teammate. How much you trust their professional judgement. How often you ask them for help or an opinion. How openly you can talk with them about work matters. Whether you would involve them before an important decision. How you would describe the joint work overall.

The result is not a single number. It is a network in which we see where trust ties converge and where the fabric thins out. This is where the small team's advantage really shows. The twenty directed ties of a five-person team fit on one diagram. The three hundred and eighty ties of a twenty-person team do not.

De Jong, Dirks and Gillespie's 2016 meta-analysis, drawing on 112 studies, showed that intrateam trust is positively and moderately related to team performance. The relationship was stronger where members' work depended more on each other. In a small team that interdependence is almost always high.

## Estimate and measurement in the same picture

trita gives an estimate of relationship dynamics even before a trust round exists. The estimate starts from the differences between personality profiles. Misunderstanding is more likely between two people with very different patterns, and the interface flags this. The estimate always appears with a source label: you can see that you are looking at a picture computed from profiles, and that measured data requires a trust round.

As soon as measured data exists, it overrides the estimate. This order never reverses. A measured, good trust relationship between two people with different profiles is a working relationship. The picture shows it that way, and the estimated risk disappears.

## What not to read out of a small team's picture

Do not read individual results out of it. The aggregate picture and the network are built so that no single answer can be traced back. When the team is small, the consultant checks this separately and, where necessary, merges or omits sensitive parts.

Do not read the future out of it. The measurement describes one point in time. A departure or a new joiner rearranges half the relationships in a four-person team. That is why, before a second measurement, we always check whether the composition has changed.

Do not read a ranking out of it. A team member with fewer incoming trust ties may be a new joiner, may work at another site, or may have work that depends less on the others. The network offers a question, not a verdict.

## Sources and limitations

The trust round and the relationship estimate are our own product solutions. The research cited supports the role of group size and trust in general, not our instrument. The effect sizes are average associations and cannot be transferred directly to a specific team.

The trust network is one layer of the team picture, alongside team roles and psychological safety. What the three layers show together is summarised on our [team diagnostics page](/team-dynamics). If measuring psychological safety interests you, read [our article on it](/blog/can-psychological-safety-be-measured).

- [De Jong, Dirks and Gillespie (2016): meta-analysis of intrateam trust and team performance, Journal of Applied Psychology](https://doi.org/10.1037/apl0000110)
- [Wheelan (2009): group size, group development and group productivity, Small Group Research](https://doi.org/10.1177/1046496408328703)
- [Mueller (2012): why individuals in larger teams perform worse, Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.obhdp.2011.08.004)

*A note on the references: Wheelan's sample consisted largely of US organisational groups, and size category boundaries differ between studies. The three-to-eight band is her division, not a general rule.*

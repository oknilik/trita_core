## Internal candidates aren’t “risk-free” either - the risks are simply different

When hiring externally, the organisation usually has to infer from interviews, references, and brief selection exercises how someone will actually perform within our team. With an internal move, there is already shared experience, the organisational context is known, and the team member’s network is not built from scratch.

One downside of external recruiting is that a candidate’s prior experience does not in itself make their success predictable. In several years of personnel data from a US financial-services firm, Matthew Bidwell found that employees hired externally into the same roles performed worse during their first two years, left more often, and initially received salaries about 18 percent higher than those promoted from within. Because the study was limited to a single organisation and sector, its findings cannot be generalised to every company. They do, however, indicate that external experience does not eliminate the uncertainty of whether a hire will work out, and can come with weaker initial results despite a higher cost.

<Callout>
Internal mobility can support retention when it offers genuine choice and a visible development path. Without the team member’s consent, however, it is not an opportunity but a transfer - and can easily have the opposite effect.
</Callout>

## What supports a well-founded internal move?

An individual personality or team-role profile belongs to the team member. It can play a part in a development conversation if they choose to use it; the manager cannot see it or interpret it on their behalf. An internal move should begin with the team member's intent, demonstrated work experience, and the actual requirements of the new role.

Current performance is important information, but the new role may require different skills. It is worth reviewing together which tasks the person has learned quickly before, where they want to develop, and what support the move would require. Understanding the receiving team's work, ways of working, and relationship context matters just as much.

## How can the aggregate team report help?

The aggregate report does not select internal candidates or reveal any team member's individual profile. At team level, it may show that certain forms of contribution are rare, that there is wide internal diversity in some working styles, or that the distribution of roles and knowledge deserves attention. These are organisational questions, not statements about individuals.

The report can help the team discuss where knowledge transfer, task sharing, or new development opportunities may be needed. It should not be used to infer which person might want which opportunity. That conversation starts with the person concerned, the goals they choose to share, and their actual work experience.

<KeyInsight>
The aggregate report raises team-level questions. An individual conversation about internal mobility begins with the team member's intent and demonstrated experience, not a profile that remains private to them.
</KeyInsight>

## A smaller step, a more manageable risk: the trial

Before a permanent transfer, it is worth offering a lower-stakes, time-limited opportunity: a project, new responsibilities undertaken for part of the team member’s working time, or a supported rotation. Clarify in advance the purpose of the trial, what the team member would like to learn, what support they will receive, what evidence you will use to evaluate the outcome together, and how they can return to their original role without disadvantage if another direction proves more suitable.

Internal mobility is therefore not a substitute for external recruiting, but an additional transparent option. Its value lies not in the move itself, but in basing the decision on more information, involving the person concerned, and following a clear learning process.

## Sources and limitations

The Bidwell study draws on one organisation and sector - instructive, but not automatically projectable onto every employer. The aggregate team report does not rank internal candidates and is not suitable for transfer decisions. An individual profile should enter a development conversation only if the team member chooses to use it.

- [Bidwell, M. (2011): Paying More to Get Less: The Effects of External Hiring versus Internal Mobility](https://doi.org/10.1177/0001839211433562). A study based on data from one US financial-services organisation.
- [CIPD: Employee turnover and retention](https://www.cipd.org/en/knowledge/factsheets/turnover-retention-factsheet/). A practical summary of retention as the joint management of several factors.
- [Ray, C. (2024): Internal Mobility: A Review and Agenda for Future Research](https://doi.org/10.1177/01492063231180826). A review organising more than two hundred studies on the forms and varied consequences of internal mobility.

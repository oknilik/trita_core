## The most expensive solution: always hiring from outside

SHRM's 2026 Talent Trends report, based on responses from more than two thousand HR professionals, paints a stark picture: 68% struggle to recruit, and 80% say candidates capable of complex problem-solving are missing. Persistent labor shortages across many markets only sharpen this – retention has moved from a cost question to a strategic one.

The same report contains a far less quoted number: internal rotation programs are 93% effective at closing talent gaps – while fewer than 25% of organizations use them.

<Callout>
In most organizations, the next key person is already inside the organization. They're just sitting in the wrong role.
</Callout>

## Why internal movement stays underused

What's missing isn't intent – it's visibility. A leader sees what someone does today, not what they'd be capable of in a different role. Performance reviews measure performance in the current role, so they're silent on exactly what a mobility decision needs: the person's natural way of contributing.

This is where team role measurement comes in. Trita's team role model describes, across nine roles, how someone naturally contributes to shared work – and the team-level picture also shows which roles are missing or overrepresented in a team.

## Three signs the answer is in-house

**A role missing in one team, surplus in another.** When one team's role matrix lacks what a neighboring team has three of, the solution is not a job ad – it's a conversation.

**A gap between self-image and team-image.** When teammates consistently see someone as strong in a different role than the one they see in themselves, that's often a sign of untapped potential – a contribution the environment notices but the current role never calls on.

**A strong measured role the current position doesn't use.** A persistent tension between someone's measured role profile and their actual job is one of the earliest, still-manageable signals of attrition risk.

<KeyInsight>
Internal mobility is neither a reward nor a last resort – it is the only recruiting channel where you already have reliable data about the candidate.
</KeyInsight>

## A cost argument, not HR poetry

An external hire costs money from the job ad through onboarding to months of ramp-up – all spent on an unknown candidate. With an internal move, ramp-up is shorter, cultural fit is known, and to the person being moved it sends the strongest retention message there is: there's room to grow here.

Measurement exists so this decision doesn't ride on gut feeling. Where the team role matrix, the self-image vs. team-image comparison, and the personality profile are available together, "who should we move, and where?" gets a data-based answer – with zero recruiting spend.

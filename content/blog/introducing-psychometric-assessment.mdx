The fate of an assessment is decided before it is announced. If participants cannot see the purpose, the path the data takes, and the possible consequences, completing it becomes an obligation and the results are distorted. According to the 2004 meta-analysis by Hausknecht and colleagues, reactions to selection procedures are strongly influenced by the perceived fairness and transparency of the procedure.

## Resistance comes from uncertainty

„What will it be used for?” „Who will see it?” „Could this have consequences for my work?” These are not irrational questions. In a workplace setting, assessment results are sensitive data. And there is a power difference between a manager and a team member to begin with.

<Callout>
Do not start with the how, start with the purpose. First you have to discuss and settle which team question you are seeking an answer to. Then it matters just as much what the results cannot be used for. The order matters too.
</Callout>

## A few questions to settle before the announcement

- What exactly is the purpose of the assessment?
- Who sees the individual, the aggregated, and the raw data?
- How long will the results be kept, and how can deletion be requested?
- What feedback do the team and the individual participants receive?
- Which HR decisions must the results never be used for?
- How can someone decline to take part, or step back later, without disadvantage?

Have your answers ready before the announcement. If even one is missing, say honestly that it is still open.

## Who can see which results?

<ResultAccessFigure locale="en" />

This is trita's default arrangement. In practice more than one solution occurs, and the right choice depends on the purpose, the team's situation, and the data protection framework. The three most common:

**Only the participant sees their own result.** The leader and the team get an aggregate view. This is the most protective arrangement, and it still works for individual development. In exchange, the team conversation will be less concrete.

**The participant decides about sharing.** Everyone chooses who they share their profile with. Here you have to watch for normative pressure, because group expectation easily overrides voluntariness.

**The facilitator sees everything, the team sees the aggregate.** The consultant works from the full picture, while only an aggregated view goes to the group. This model works best in sensitive situations. It requires a well-prepared facilitator and confidentiality set out in writing.

None of them is „the right” one, and research does not rank them. The arrangement in the figure above follows the third.

## Three principles for the communication

**1. This is a tool for development.** Say concretely which conversation the assessment can support, individually and at team level. Say also that a score is not a diagnosis, not a performance review, and not a verdict on suitability.

**2. Voluntariness.** A mandatory or high-stakes setup increases distortion in the answers. Voluntariness is an ethical and trust principle. It does not, however, guarantee undistorted data, because self-selection and missing answers can still occur. Anyone who does not want to take part should not take part.

**3. Data handling settled in advance.** Do not promise security in general terms. Name who can see what, as discussed above. Say also that deletion of the data can be requested from trita at any time, and that direct feedback can be sent to us.

## Five steps of the introduction

**1. Define the purpose and the limits, on your own or together.** Write in one sentence which question you are seeking an answer to. It is enough if you simply want to know yourselves better, and to understand the dynamics you work along. Then talk about what you will not use the results for.

**2. Give the information before the decision.** Before the invitation to participate, there should be a clear description of the process, the data, and the option to decline.

**3. Set an example without pressure.** Sharing your own result can be a credible gesture. No expectation towards the others may follow from it.

**4. Leave the right to share intact.** Do not ask for individual profiles to be made public. Not sharing remains a decision to be respected later too.

**5. Interpret the results with a facilitator.** The length of the workshop is determined by team size and purpose. Keep the focus on concrete cooperation situations.

Trita gives you a shared vocabulary when the results produce questions, concrete plans for working together, and agreements. Plain labelling leads to nothing lasting.

## A template for the announcement

> I would like to start a voluntary assessment, at both the personal and the team level. Our aim is to give you a chance to get to know yourselves, to gather feedback, and to sharpen and refine the way we work together.
>
> The personality assessment takes about ten minutes. Together with the team-level questionnaires it comes to roughly thirty, spread across blocks of a few minutes each. Everyone sees their own result and no one else's. Only an aggregated view goes to the team, with no individual scores. I will not see anyone's individual result either.
>
> We will not use the results in pay decisions or individual performance reviews. Declining to take part has no consequences whatsoever, and you do not owe me a reason. The data will be kept for [X] months, and deletion can be requested at any time.
>
> We will go through the results together in a facilitated conversation on [date]. If you would like to ask anything beforehand, please do.

Rewrite this template for your own situation.

## What about during a conflict?

In an acute conflict, publicly sharing individual profiles is especially risky. Scores easily turn into personal accusations. This does not mean all measurement is forbidden at such times. With a clear purpose, explicit consent, aggregated data, and a well-prepared facilitator, the process can support the work of surfacing what is going on.

It is not only the Honesty-Humility and Agreeableness dimensions that are sensitive. Any personal result can be used for labelling if a shared interpretive and data-handling frame is missing.

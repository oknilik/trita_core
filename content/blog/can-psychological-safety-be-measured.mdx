## What does psychological safety give a team?

Psychological safety expresses how safe team members feel asking questions, admitting mistakes, requesting help, and voicing professional disagreement. It does not mean comfort, and it does not mean the absence of conflict - it means the important things get said.

<Callout>
In a psychologically safe team, professional disagreements surface more openly, because members spend less energy on self-protection.
</Callout>

The research picture links psychological safety with learning behaviour, information sharing, and speaking up - and, more moderately, with performance. This is why it deserves a leader's attention: it is the channel through which bad news still reaches you in time.

## What did Google's research show?

Project Aristotle examined 180 of Google's own teams and highlighted psychological safety first among the five dynamics associated with effective teams. The lesson is not that knowledge doesn't matter - it is that even excellent individual capabilities pay off through everyday interactions. (An internal, observational analysis: strong as guidance, not a law of nature.)

## How does trita estimate it?

Psychological safety can be estimated from team members' perceptions - with a questionnaire. Amy Edmondson's original seven-item scale asks about the handling of mistakes, difficult topics, and asking for help; trita's short survey consists of eight statements, builds on that conceptual frame, and is its own instrument.

We are open about this. We document the scale's psychometric properties - reliability, factor structure, team-level aggregation - as the Hungarian data accumulates, and we publish the results on this blog. Until then, the index works as an estimate: a reference point for the team's own conversation.

<KeyInsight>
The index aggregates team members' perceptions: it shows how safe speaking up appears to be. That is exactly the data a leader finds hardest to get directly - whoever doesn't feel safe won't say so out loud.
</KeyInsight>

## How does trita protect the responses?

trita handles short-survey responses without direct personal identifiers, and shows no team index below three responses. For small teams we choose our words precisely: we promise confidential, identifier-free data handling and a minimum reporting threshold - not “total anonymity”. Honest answers need both things: understandable data handling, and the experience that answers carry no personal consequences.

## From index to leadership conversation

The index is not a ready-made instruction - it is a starting point. In a consulting process we interpret it together with the team's own experience and its concrete workflows, and small, testable changes come out of that. A few experiments that work:

- **Learning reviews of mistakes.** In the retro, first look at what a mistake taught you - only then at how to fix it.
- **Voicing leader uncertainty.** An “I'm not sure about this - what do you see?” opens more than a ready answer.
- **Inviting the minority view.** Before a decision, ask by name the person who hasn't spoken yet.
- **Making help-seeking simple.** Give it an explicit channel and time - it shouldn't be a test of courage.

These are not recipes but experiments to adapt to the team's situation - the index helps you choose which one to start with.

## What does a change between two measurements mean?

A second measurement by itself gives you a number - which is why trita's comparison report shows the score difference together with measurement uncertainty and changes in who responded. If the difference stays within that range, we do not claim improvement or decline. If it goes beyond it, you have a real topic for the next conversation: what happened between the two measurements?

## Sources and limitations

The index is a self-report estimate: it aggregates team members' perceptions and is not an objective rating of the team or its leader. The three-response threshold is a risk-reducing product rule, not a scientifically established anonymity guarantee - in very small teams, re-identification cannot be fully excluded. The Google result is one organisation's internal analysis and does not generalise automatically; psychological safety is an important factor, but not the sole determinant of performance. Trust in the process is a precondition of honest answers, but does not by itself guarantee the measure's validity.

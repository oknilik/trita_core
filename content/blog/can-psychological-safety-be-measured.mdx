## What is psychological safety?

Psychological safety describes how safe team members feel asking questions, admitting mistakes, seeking help, and voicing professional disagreement. It does not mean comfort or an absence of conflict.

<Callout>
Professional disagreement may surface more openly in a psychologically safe team because members need to spend less energy on self-protection.
</Callout>

The research evidence links psychological safety with learning behaviour, information sharing, voice, and – more modestly – performance. It is an important team factor, but it is not universally “the strongest predictor” and does not determine performance on its own.

## What did Google's study show?

Project Aristotle examined 180 teams inside Google. Psychological safety was the first of five important dynamics associated with effective team functioning. This is a valuable organisational case study, but it is an internal, observational analysis and cannot be generalised automatically to every organisation.

The study does not show that team composition or individual expertise are irrelevant. A better interpretation is that excellent individual capability may not be fully used when interaction quality is poor.

## How can it be estimated?

Psychological safety is typically estimated from team members' self-reports. Amy Edmondson's original seven-item scale asks how members perceive the treatment of mistakes, difficult issues, help-seeking, and interpersonal risk.

trita's own short pulse contains eight statements and starts from Edmondson's construct. It is not identical to the original scale. To describe it as an independently validated measure, trita must document item origin and adaptation, reliability, factor structure, justification for team-level aggregation, and sensitivity to change.

<KeyInsight>
trita's index estimates team members' perceived psychological safety. It is not an objective rating of the team or its leader.
</KeyInsight>

## What does the `n ≥ 3` threshold mean?

trita handles pulse responses without a direct personal identifier and does not display a team index below three responses. This reduces the risk of directly exposing individual answers.

Three responses are not a scientifically established anonymity boundary. In a small team, wording, timing, or context may still allow inference. It is more accurate to describe confidential, non-directly-identifying data handling and a minimum reporting threshold than to promise “full anonymity”.

Credible response collection requires participants to understand the data flow, trust the process, and not expect personal consequences. This matters, but it does not guarantee validity on its own.

## From index to leadership conversation

An index is not a ready-made leadership instruction. In a consultant-led process, it can be compared with the team's experience, other aggregate evidence, and the actual workflow. This can inform the next conversation and small, testable changes.

Examples include reviewing mistakes for learning, leaders acknowledging uncertainty, deliberately inviting minority views, or making help-seeking easier. These are context-dependent experiments, not universal prescriptions.

## What does change between two rounds mean?

A second round shows a difference in the index score. Interpreting it as meaningful change requires attention to measurement error, respondent overlap, team composition, and events between rounds.

trita's comparison report therefore interprets the score difference alongside measurement uncertainty and composition change. If a difference cannot be distinguished from uncertainty, the report does not claim that psychological safety improved or declined.

When you turn a continuous scale into four letters, you lose the size of the differences. That is the most important structural difference between the six-factor model and MBTI. What is at stake becomes clear when you want to revisit your team's results a year later.

## Categories lose information

MBTI forms sixteen categories from four preference pairs. The four-letter code is easy to remember and makes a good conversation starter at a team event. The six-factor model gives a 0-100 value on six dimensions and preserves the distances.

McCrae and Costa's 1989 analysis measured the MBTI scales against continuous personality dimensions. They found that the scales behind the preference pairs show meaningful covariation with the continuous traits. The contested point in the research is the categorisation itself. Part of the information is lost in the conversion to letters.

<Callout>
The question is not which questionnaire is more serious. The question is what you want to use it for. A good conversation needs only a shared vocabulary. Understanding how your team works needs degrees and comparable scales.
</Callout>

## Two colleagues, two letters, barely different scores

Imagine two of your colleagues standing at almost the same point on the <DimBadge code="X" label="Extraversion" /> scale. They happen to sit on opposite sides of the category boundary. One gets an „E”, the other an „I”, as if they were opposites.

A third colleague stands thirty points away and still gets the same letter as one of them. The language of categories errs in both directions. It magnifies the small difference. It erases the large one.

<KeyInsight>
A graded scale shows a small difference as small and a large one as large. A few points do not yet mean a different working style. A thirty-point distance, though, is worth discussing with the team.
</KeyInsight>

## Honesty-Humility: what most questionnaires leave out

<DimBadge code="H" label="Honesty-Humility" /> is the most distinctive addition of the six-factor model. Neither the classic Big Five nor MBTI has a separate equivalent. It brings together four areas: sincerity, fairness, avoiding greed, and modesty. As a leader, this is what lets you talk about fairness, status, and the pursuit of self-interest.

In 2019 Pletzer and colleagues summarised the research on the relationship between personality and counterproductive work behaviour. Honesty-Humility showed the strongest link among the dimensions studied, at ρ = −0.44. This is a moderately strong association. In plain terms it means that comparing large groups, groups with higher scores show on average fewer norm violations. Many individual cases run counter to that tendency.

The 2019 meta-analysis by Lee, Berry and Gonzalez-Mulé measured the relationship with task performance. There the figure was ρ = 0.15. This is a weak association. In practice it means you can barely infer professional performance from a personality score.

What follows in practice is that where internal diversity is large, it is worth talking about fairness and shared rules. The aggregate report does not show where anyone stands on the dimension. That is deliberate. We write about it in detail in our [article on the aggregate team profile](/blog/reading-your-teams-personality-profile).

## Which one, when?

**As a one-off conversation starter, MBTI works too.** If the goal is a shared vocabulary for a self-awareness workshop, the language of categories draws participants in quickly. People enjoy it, and that is not nothing.

**Teamwork and later comparison need graded dimensions.** This matters if you want to build on the results later. You might compare them with colleague feedback, look at the distribution across a team, or repeat the measurement a year from now. All three need a measurement that preserves the size of the differences.

The publisher of MBTI also reports reliability data. The decisive difference is structural. A graded scale preserves more of what you set out to measure.

## Four questions to ask a questionnaire vendor

If you are choosing a questionnaire for your team, ask the vendor these four questions. The answers tell you more than the marketing material.

**„Does the result give a degree or a category?”** If a category, ask what happens to people standing near the boundary.

**„In which language and on what sample was it validated?”** A Hungarian translation of an instrument validated in English is not automatically a validated instrument. Adaptation is separate work.

**„What does the reliability data show, and where does it come from?”** Ask for a specific figure and a source. „Backed by research” on its own means nothing.

**„What can the result not be used for?”** A well-prepared vendor answers this quickly and concretely. If they have no answer, that is information in itself.

## Limitations and sources

The ρ values above are corrected, group-level meta-analytic averages. They show tendencies and do not predict individual behaviour. A low Honesty-Humility score does not prove unethical conduct, and on its own is not suitable for any HR decision.

In self-report form, both approaches can be distorted by response style and social desirability. Research findings about a model family do not automatically apply to every specific questionnaire. High-stakes use requires validation for the given purpose. Trita's 0-100 scale is a display format, and interpreting it also requires knowing the norms and the measurement uncertainty.

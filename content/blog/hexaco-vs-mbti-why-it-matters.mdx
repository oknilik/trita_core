## Both have their place - but not for the same job

Almost everyone knows MBTI. The four-letter code is memorable, and it makes a great icebreaker at a team day. HEXACO offers something different: it shows how, and how much, team members differ along six dimensions, in degrees.

<Callout>
The question is not which test is “better”. The question is what you want to use it for: a good conversation only needs a shared language - understanding how your team works needs degrees and comparable scales.
</Callout>

## Type or degree?

Imagine two colleagues standing almost at the same point on the Extraversion scale - but just either side of the type boundary. One gets an “E”, the other an “I”, as if they were opposites. A third colleague, 30 points away, gets the same letter as one of them. The type language loses information in both directions: it magnifies the small difference and hides the large one.

<KeyInsight>
A graded scale shows a small difference as small and a large one as large. A few points apart is not yet a difference in working style - a 30-point distance, though, gives your next team conversation a real topic.
</KeyInsight>

This is MBTI's most debated point in the research: converting continuous differences into letter categories. Not because the underlying scales measure nothing - but because categorisation throws part of the information away.

## Honesty-Humility: what most tests leave out

<DimBadge code="H" label="Honesty-Humility" /> is HEXACO's most distinctive addition - neither the classic Big Five nor MBTI has a standalone equivalent. It covers four areas: sincerity, fairness, greed avoidance, and modesty.

Through a leader's eyes, this dimension gives you language for fairness, status, and self-interest. According to meta-analyses, of the personality dimensions studied it shows the strongest link with counterproductive work behaviour: higher scores go with fewer norm violations at group level - that is the ρ = −.44 above, a moderately strong and consistently replicated association. Its link with task performance, by contrast, is weak: personality is no substitute for professional skill.

In practice, if the aggregate team report indicates wide internal diversity on Honesty-Humility, it is worth opening a conversation about fairness and shared rules. The report does not reveal where any individual stands on the dimension. We cover this in detail in [our article on aggregate team profiles](/blog/reading-your-teams-personality-profile).

## When to use which

**For a one-off conversation starter: MBTI.** If the goal is a shared vocabulary for a reflective workshop, the type language draws people in quickly.

**For team work and follow-up: graded dimensions.** If you want to build on the results later - compare them with colleague feedback, look at the team-level distribution, or revisit them in a year - you need a measure that preserves the size of the differences.

HEXACO has a strong research base as a model, and MBTI's publisher also reports reliability data. The decisive difference is structural: a graded scale keeps more of what you are trying to measure.

## Sources and limitations

The ρ values are corrected, group-level meta-analytic averages: they show tendencies and do not predict an individual's behaviour. A low Honesty-Humility score does not prove unethical conduct and is not suitable for any HR decision on its own. In self-report form, both approaches can be affected by response style and social desirability. Evidence for the model family does not transfer automatically to a specific questionnaire: high-stakes use requires purpose-specific validation. trita's 0-100 scale is a display format - interpretation also requires norms and measurement uncertainty.
